## Voice laws (how an A Story line is written)

1. **Say the unsaid thing, simply.** A real person saying it to their person — not a
   poet performing. If it sounds composed, it's wrong.
2. **Spoken, not literary.** Short. Hinglish or English someone would actually text
   or mutter. (*"main theek hoon"*, not "the quiet ache of being unseen.")
3. **A turn, not a statement.** Reframe the obvious: *not asking* = higher care;
   *"I'm fine"* ≠ fine; *talking too much* = being deeply listened to.
4. **A specific receipt, not a general claim.** One concrete object/gesture proves
   it — chai, the charger, the held shoes, the pillow. Never "small things matter."
5. **Earn the send.** The reader must want to send it to one specific person. If no
   one comes to mind, it failed.
6. **The story chooses its length.** 1 illustration or 10 — whatever the feeling
   needs. A single illustration is valid and often strongest.

**Never:** therapy-page/motivational lines; "communication is key"; over-poetry;
"people think… but actually…"; writing to *complete a template*. Patterns are
monotony; craft is range.

## The two valid origins (never a third)

1. **Remix a specific proven story** — a winner, a song line, a pop-culture pairing,
   a metaphor — + one true human twist (the pillow law).
2. **Originate from one true unsaid feeling** using the craft.

Never invent generic poetry. Never templatize a winner into a formula. The test
before anything ships: *would this move someone who has seen 50 A Story posts this
week — or would they feel the formula?*
