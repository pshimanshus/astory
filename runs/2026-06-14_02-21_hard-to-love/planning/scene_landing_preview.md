## Recommended Idea

### The Chair Outside The Door

Score: `4.86 / 5`

Exact first-slide on-image text:

`am i hard to love when i go quiet?`

First-frame visual:

An airy off-white watercolor hallway. A closed door sits slightly to one side;
a thin strip of warm light under it says someone is inside without showing a
face. A simple empty chair waits outside the door, close enough to be found but
not pressed against it. The frame has generous quiet negative space for the
handwritten question. No dramatic darkness. No forced couple close-up.

Why the first swipe happens:

The viewer wants to know whether the person outside knocks, leaves, or learns
the right kind of closeness.

Mini slide arc:

1. A closed door and practical chair ask: `am i hard to love when i go quiet?`
2. Zuv's hand places water and a charger where the door will not hit them, then
   leaves the frame.
3. Time passes through object logic: the chair shifts to the right distance,
   the folded cloth appears, and the light under the door changes.
4. The door is cracked open; the water is half empty and the charger is in use.
5. Payoff: the cloth or charger has been pulled inside, and the empty glass or
   moved chair quietly proves she was not abandoned while quiet.

Payoff frame:

The door is open just enough to show care was accepted. The changed objects,
not a hug or speech, carry the answer: half-empty glass, charger crossing the
threshold, folded cloth moved, chair still nearby but not heroic.

Why someone sends it:

"This is me when I disappear but still want to be found gently." A partner can
send it as a thank-you or a soft request without starting a heavy argument.

What could make it land flat/generic:

It becomes generic if it is just a sad person behind a door with poetic text, or
if Zuv is staged like a perfect martyr waiting all night.

Correction if it feels flat:

Make the objects do the emotional work: chair distance, glass level, charger
path, folded cloth, door crack, and light should all visibly change. Keep both
people partial or absent where the objects are enough.

## Alternatives

### The Little Instructions He Learned

- Exact first-slide text: `am i hard to handle, or did you learn me gently?`
- First-frame visual: Aachu alone near an open doorway; water, charger, blanket,
  and low light are already arranged around her before the text explains them.
- Mini arc: she fears she is too much; the room reveals tiny care rules he
  remembers; he gives space without disappearing; she accepts one care object;
  the ordinary setup becomes the answer.
- Payoff frame: Aachu touches the water glass or blanket, softer now, with Zuv
  only implied by the care he left.
- Share trigger: "you know my care instructions."
- Flat/generic risk: It can become a care-label meme or make Aachu look like a
  difficult object with settings.
- Correction: Use the behavior, not the wording. Make one care rule weirdly
  specific and couple-owned.

### The Bag That Knew Already

- Exact first-slide text: `am i hard to handle, or do i come with instructions?`
- First-frame visual: An open sling bag on warm off-white paper: water bottle,
  hair tie, small snack, charger, folded scarf. Aachu and Zuv do not need to
  appear in the opener.
- Mini arc: the bag introduces the care-instructions idea; Aachu is at the edge
  of a noisy place; one object appears in use; Zuv is partly shown making space;
  the used bag sits between them in a quieter corner.
- Payoff frame: The bag becomes evidence that her needs were expected, not
  tolerated.
- Share trigger: "my care label says snacks first, questions later."
- Flat/generic risk: It can become Pinterest care-kit romance if objects are too
  aesthetic or too many.
- Correction: Use only four to six lived-in objects and make one object matter
  twice.
