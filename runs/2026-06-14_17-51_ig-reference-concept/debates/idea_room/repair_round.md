# Idea Room Repair Round

Run: `2026-06-14_17-51_ig-reference-concept`

## Selected Raw Direction

`The Hair Clip In His Pocket`

Raw premise: Zuv remembers that Aachu always needs a hair clip at the exact moment she has already given up pretending her hair is fine.

## Slop Risks Found

- `AI_SLOP_COPY_DRIFT`: could collapse into "he remembers the little things."
- `VISUAL_GENERIC_RISK`: could become a pretty hair-fixing pose.
- `WEAK_PAYOFF`: could end in a hug instead of the remembered object.
- `SCENE_LOGIC_CONTRADICTION`: the clip must plausibly be carried in a pocket, wallet, key loop, or small bag.
- `STEREOTYPE_RISK`: Aachu must not be helpless; Zuv must not be saintly.

## Repairs Applied

- Make Zuv the carrier of the object, not the fixer of Aachu.
- Keep the object specific: an old black clip, slightly unromantic, the kind she actually loses.
- Let Aachu clip her own hair, slightly crooked, so the power of the scene is being remembered, not being styled.
- Make Zuv underplay the act. He should look like someone returning to his food, not someone waiting for applause.
- Avoid using the reference line `"Of course I did."` as the payoff. Use everyday speech instead.
- Keep first-slide text raw and accusatory: `why do you have my clip?`

## Repaired Concept

Title: `The Hair Clip He Started Carrying`

Concept: Aachu reaches for a hair clip right as the food arrives, and Zuv already has her exact old clip in his pocket because he knows this tiny chaos happens every time.

Emotional job: Let someone send the carousel to the partner who knows their tiny emergency object without having to say "thank you for noticing me."

Visual proof: hair in face, hands occupied, Zuv's hand offering the old clip, the open pocket/wallet/key loop as evidence, Aachu's half-annoyed/half-soft expression.

Payoff: She clips her hair herself and catches that he has been carrying her tiny problem around like normal inventory.

## Final Check

This stays close to the reference mechanic while refusing the reference format. It is not quote-card intimacy; it is object-led couple memory.
