# Shareability Strategist - Kabir

Slide 1 stays locked:

`Khaali hai jo tere bina, main woh ghar hoon tera.`

## Rejected Stale Version

The old draft was still explaining the line like a caption writer:

> Log samajhte hain ghar woh hota hai jahan hum laut ke aate hain...

Problem: it starts with a thesis. Nobody sends a thesis to their person at night.
It also keeps defining home from the outside. The better post has to sound like
the house/person finally saying the one thing they usually swallow.

Hard rejects:
- "People think..." setup.
- "You feel like home."
- "Tere bina main bas ek jagah hoon."
- Any line that teaches the meaning of ghar.

## Source Mechanics Used

- `thirdeyethoughts / DZTTSUBEaRr`: ordinary care as love without saying "I love you." Use the tiny receipt: chabi ki awaaz, "aa gaya", door opening.
- `werenotreallystrangers / DW9g9ZGCRu-`: partner-as-home is the familiar surface. We use it only as the thing to beat.
- `atticuspoetry / DYG9a-FEuBQ`: deep feeling sits inside ordinary things. No dramatic loneliness. Just one normal sound changing the room.
- Winner-bank pattern: home works only when grounded. A door, key, side of bed, or arrival line has to carry the emotion.
- Novelty model: not "you are my home." More painful: "main ghar hoon, par tere aane se hi ghar lagta hoon."

## Top Pick

Candidate 2.

It is the least written. It starts with boring normal things, then one arrival
proof changes the feeling. The line "main tera wait kar raha tha" is the send
trigger because it says the needy thing without sounding needy.

## Why It Gets Sent

Someone sends this when they miss their person but do not want to write a long
"I miss you" paragraph. The receipt is small enough to feel real: the key, the
door, the "aa gaya." It lets the sender say, "come home, I become myself when
you arrive," without turning it into a polished confession.
